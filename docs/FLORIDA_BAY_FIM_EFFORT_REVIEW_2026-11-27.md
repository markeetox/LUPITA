# Florida Bay FWC/FMRI Fisheries-Independent Monitoring Review

**Review date:** 2026-11-27  
**Target taxon:** Southern flounder (*Paralichthys lethostigma*)  
**Purpose:** Audit the survey design and public data trail behind the reported 1994–1997 Florida Bay non-detections. Read-only research only; no species profile, geographic mask, scoring, or production behavior changed.

## Executive finding

GSMFC Publication No. 247 reports that no Southern flounder were collected in routine monthly fisheries-independent monitoring samples in Florida Bay over 1994–1997, citing unpublished FWC/FMRI data. The statement is supported by a real, documented monitoring program, but the underlying species-by-sample catch table was not located in the public sources reviewed. The published evidence is therefore a secondary report of an unpublished non-detection result, not an independently re-counted raw-data result.

A 2019 FWC/FWRI-authored SEDAR data-workshop paper provides useful sampling-design and effort context:

- Fixed-station FIM sampling operated at 31 stations inside Everglades National Park boundaries from 1994–1997.
- Three samples per site per month were collected from August 1994 through December 1995; sampling then reduced to one sample per site per month.
- One station was dropped in January 1997, leaving 30 stations through October 1997.
- The paper reports 2,636 fixed-station samples: 1,367 seine samples and 1,269 trawl samples.
- Stratified-random sampling (SRS) also began in Florida Bay in 1997. The paper reports 27 sets per month across five ecological zones; in 1997, 130 seines and 140 trawls were completed.
- Florida Bay sampling stopped after October 1997 because the Everglades National Park collecting permit was not renewed.

These totals describe broad multi-species survey effort. They do not establish that every sample was equally suitable for detecting Southern flounder, and they do not substitute for the species-by-sample catch data. The SEDAR paper is a yellowtail snapper study and does not publish a Southern flounder catch table.



## Additional confirmation from later FWC annual reports | 2026-11-27

The 2021 and 2023 FWC/FWRI FIM Annual Data Summary Reports independently state that Florida Bay sampling occurred from 1993 through 1997. The 2021 report describes its then-current monthly SRS monitoring areas as Tampa Bay, Charlotte Harbor, northern and southern Indian River Lagoon, Cedar Key, Apalachicola Bay, and northeast Florida; Florida Bay is described only as a historical 1993–1997 program. The 2023 report likewise lists Florida Bay only in the historical sampling summary and its current core-sampling estuary sections do not include Florida Bay. This corroborates the end of the Florida Bay FIM period, but neither report publishes the underlying 1994–1997 Southern flounder sample-level catch records.

The 2023 report's bibliography explicitly cites three primary FIM annual summaries that should be requested from FWRI:
- FDEP-FMRI (1995), *Fisheries-Independent Monitoring program 1994 annual data summary report*.
- FDEP-FMRI (1997), *Fisheries-Independent Monitoring program 1996 annual data summary report*.
- FDEP-FMRI (1998), *Fisheries-Independent Monitoring program 1997 annual data summary report*.

The 1995 annual summary for 1994 is 451 pages; this page count is independently given in a NOAA technical memorandum's bibliography. A separate Florida Bay juvenile-fish publication also cites the 1995 and 1996 annual summaries, providing a second bibliographic trail. The reports have not yet been obtained or checked for Southern flounder tables. Their existence is a retrieval lead, not confirmation of the GSMFC species-specific non-detection statement.

**Acquisition priority:** request the three original annual reports, plus the full fixed-station and 1997 SRS sample/gear and species-catch extracts. Request all samples including zero-catch and invalid deployments, taxonomic codes, lengths, sample effort, habitat/depth, and QA/QC fields. The 2019 SEDAR summary and later FWC annual reports establish survey design and program history only; they cannot replace the original sample-level denominator and catch table.

## Survey design and interpretation

The fixed-station design sampled a predetermined network of sites. FWC/FWRI methods documentation explains that fixed-station samples describe observations at the sampled sites and cannot be statistically expanded to the entire estuary in the same way as stratified-random samples. SRS sampling began alongside fixed stations in Florida Bay in 1997, but was cut short in October of that year.

The early 1995 program description reports 31 monthly fixed stations, with 16 seine sites and 15 trawl sites. It describes shallow-water seine sampling and otter trawls in deeper water, with environmental measurements including temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity, depth, and bottom/habitat observations.

This matters for Southern flounder because a non-detection must be interpreted in relation to gear, depth, habitat, season, sample location, and actual effort. A seine sample, a short shallow-water trawl, and a shelf bottom trawl do not have interchangeable detection probabilities.

## Public FWC GIS layer: useful but not the raw negative-sample table

FWC metadata describes a statewide FIM stratified-random sampling point layer with 1,765,226 features and a source period beginning 1996. The layer is intended to document locations, dates, numbers, and species caught. However, its metadata explicitly states that the GIS export was restricted to records with CNT > 0.

Consequences:
- The layer can provide positive-catch records and survey context.
- A missing Southern flounder record in this positive-catch-only export cannot by itself establish a zero catch.
- It cannot independently reconstruct the full sample denominator or all no-catch events.
- Its period begins in 1996 and the published metadata describes an export ending in 2016; it is not a substitute for the full 1994–1997 Florida Bay fixed-station dataset.

The FWC metadata says the source tabular data are held by FWRI Saltwater FIM and recommends obtaining the data directly from FWC. It lists the GIS Librarian contact for that GIS product, but the raw FIM catch/sample data should be requested from the FIM program.

## Data access and requests needed

### FWC/FWRI

Request the original Florida Bay FIM sample-level dataset for 1994–1997, including both fixed-station and 1997 stratified-random sampling. Request:

1. Every sample/gear deployment, including zero-catch and unsuccessful/invalid samples.
2. Sample date/time, station or zone, coordinates, depth, habitat, and environmental measurements.
3. Gear type, deployment duration/distance, effort, replicate identifier, and any quality/validity flags.
4. Species-level catch records, including taxonomic codes, counts, lengths, and the coding convention for zero catch versus no record.
5. The exact data extract or analysis underlying the GSMFC statement that no Southern flounder were collected during 1994–1997.
6. Program methods, QA/QC documentation, and any annual summaries or unpublished technical reports for Florida Bay.

The 2014 FIM metadata note says the detailed program data summary reports are prepared annually and earlier reports are available on request. The 2019 SEDAR paper provides the Florida Bay design and effort context, but not the target species catch table.

### Florida Museum of Natural History

The public BioGator listing currently provides the following core details for the priority records:

- UF 164232: March 1962, Buttonwood Canal, Everglades National Park, Monroe County; collector shown as unknown.
- UF 142820: 1960-12-26, Port Everglades turning basin, Broward County; collector John Coles.
- UF 233907: 1959-04-25, Port Everglades, southwest corner near Intracoastal Waterway, Broward County; collector John Coles.

The Florida Museum's Ichthyology collection page directs comments, inquiries, and requests for complete records, including specific locality information, to Collection Manager Rob Robins (rhrobins@flmnh.ufl.edu).

Request for each catalog number:
- Complete Specify record and all verbatim locality fields.
- Collector field number, field notes, collecting event and associated lot/specimen count.
- Original label text and any retained collector/determiner inserts.
- Identification history, determiner, and date of determination.
- Any digitized images or scans of the specimen and original labels.
- Preservation/condition notes and whether the lot remains extant.

The museum's collection standards explain that specimen lots are linked to collection information through collector field numbers and that former labels and collector/determiner inserts are retained during curation. This makes the original label and field-number trail especially useful for validating the Buttonwood Canal and Port Everglades records.

## Current interpretation for LUPITA

1. Retain the Florida Bay 1994–1997 non-detection as a historical, source-attributed survey result, not as a hard absence boundary.
2. Do not treat the 2,636 fixed-station samples or 270 SRS sets as 2,906 independent samples without checking the underlying sample records and possible overlaps/definitions.
3. Do not infer Southern flounder zero-catch events from the positive-catch-only FWC GIS export.
4. Do not extrapolate fixed-station non-detections across all Florida Bay habitats or beyond the sampled stations.
5. Keep the Florida Bay historical survey evidence separate from NOAA/SEAMAP shelf-trawl evidence and Florida Museum voucher records.
6. Do not change the species profile, geographic mask, confidence, or scoring until the original FIM species-by-sample records and the museum's full voucher metadata are obtained and audited.
