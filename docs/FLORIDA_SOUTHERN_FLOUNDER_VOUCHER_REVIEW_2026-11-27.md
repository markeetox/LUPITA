# Florida Southern Flounder Voucher and South Florida Distribution Review

**Review date:** 2026-11-27  
**Taxon:** Southern flounder (*Paralichthys lethostigma*)  
**Purpose:** Compare the NOAA/SEAMAP shelf-trawl evidence with Florida Museum collection records and regional distribution literature. This is a read-only evidence review. No species profile, range mask, scoring, or production behavior is changed.

## Executive finding

The evidence does **not** support a hard South Florida exclusion or a simple latitude cutoff for *P. lethostigma*. The Florida Museum of Natural History's public UF Ichthyology collection search lists exact-species records from Port Everglades (Broward County) and Buttonwood Canal in Everglades National Park (Monroe County), as well as other southwest and south-central Florida localities. A Gulf States Marine Fisheries Commission (GSMFC) management profile separately reports historical specimens from the Caloosahatchee River estuary and Florida Bay, while also noting that routine Florida Bay fisheries-independent monitoring from 1994–1997 did not collect the species.

Taken together, these sources support a cautious interpretation: Southern flounder may occur in low numbers or as infrequent records in parts of South Florida, while the peninsula remains a meaningful distribution/population break. The historical records are not evidence of a dense, continuous, or currently established population throughout South Florida. They are enough to reject a hard geographic absence mask.

## Regional Gulf evidence

GSMFC Publication No. 247, *Management Profile for the Gulf and Southern Flounder Fishery in the Gulf of Mexico*, describes southern Florida distribution as uncertain and summarizes several records:

- Gunter and Hall (1965) reportedly collected two specimens in the Caloosahatchee River estuary.
- Tabb and Manning (1961) reported two southern flounder specimens from Florida Bay in Everglades National Park and noted reports from recreational anglers off local sandy beaches.
- The same GSMFC profile reports that no southern flounder were collected in Florida Bay during routine monthly fisheries-independent monitoring from 1994 through 1997 (FWC/FMRI unpublished data cited by GSMFC).
- GSMFC also reports one 315 mm standard-length specimen caught in February 1998 approximately 16 km north of Marathon in the Gulf of Mexico; the specimen was obtained from a commercial fish house in Marathon.

These records are not equivalent in evidence quality. The museum catalog entries are collection records; the Florida Bay and Caloosahatchee records are historical reports summarized by GSMFC; the 1994–1997 non-detections are described as unpublished FWC/FMRI monitoring data in that profile. They should remain separate source classes.

## Distribution summaries and apparent disagreement

- The Florida Museum species page summarizes Florida distribution as western and north-central drainages and the Atlantic Coast. It does not list South Florida in that short range summary, while its collection search contains the Port Everglades and Buttonwood Canal records above.
- The USGS Nonindigenous Aquatic Species profile gives a native range ending at Jupiter Inlet on the Atlantic side and the Caloosahatchee estuary on the Gulf side, and says South Florida is not included in the range. This is a range-summary statement based on older references, not a complete listing of every historical specimen.
- A peer-reviewed population-genetics paper describes a break in distribution along the southern Florida peninsula. A population break is not the same claim as absolute absence of every individual south of a line.

The apparent conflict is best handled by preserving the distinction between a broad range summary, a population/connectivity break, rare historical collection records, and repeated survey non-detections.

## Implications for LUPITA

1. Do not create a hard South Florida absence polygon or a fixed latitude exclusion from these data.
2. Keep individual occurrence evidence with its source, date, locality, catalog/accession number, and evidence class.
3. Do not treat historical museum records as current abundance or as proof of a self-sustaining local population.
4. Do not treat the 1994–1997 Florida Bay non-detections as proof of permanent absence; they are bounded by survey design, time period, and sampling effort.
5. Keep the NOAA/SEAMAP bottom-trawl no-catch evidence separate from museum occurrence records. They answer different questions and have different spatial/temporal coverage.
6. Before any profile or scoring change, obtain full specimen metadata/labels for the key South Florida UF catalog entries and, if possible, the underlying FWC/FMRI Florida Bay monitoring dataset or its methods/report.
